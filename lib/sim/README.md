# lib/sim: the physics core of the vehicle simulator

The arithmetic behind `sim_vehicle` ([docs/14-simulation.md](../../docs/14-simulation.md),
"Physics"): a six-degree-of-freedom rigid-body model of the BlueROV2 heavy
configuration, the T200 thrust table and thruster allocation, the sensor noise
models, and the least-squares calibration behind `hangar sim fit`. C11, no
dependencies beyond libm, no allocation, no I/O, no topics; deterministic bit for
bit given the same inputs. The module that publishes topics (`sim_vehicle`) and
the command-line tool (`hangar sim fit`) are built on it and live elsewhere.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `sim_model.h/.c` | Fossen's equation, RK4, surface and floor, parameters with sources |
| `sim_thrust.h/.c` | T200 table, voltage interpolation, geometry, 6×8 allocation, lag, `sim_applied_wrench` |
| `sim_noise.h/.c` | xorshift64, Gaussian noise, bias, latency; IMU, depth and attitude models |
| `sim_fit.h/.c` | fit of buoyancy, damping and lag to a recording |
| `data/t200_thrust.csv` | Blue Robotics' published T200 data, as fetched |
| `data/t200_thrust.inc` | the same in newtons as a C table, generated by `data/gen_t200_inc.sh` |
| `tests/test_sim.c` | the suite, `make check-sim` |

## The model as implemented

Body frame: x forward, y right, z down, origin at the centre of gravity. World
frame: NED, z down, so `pos[2]` is depth. Attitude is a unit quaternion (w, x, y, z)
rotating body vectors into the world; ν = [u v w p q r] is the body velocity.

```
(M_RB + M_A) ν̇ + C_RB(ν) ν + C_A(ν_r) ν_r + D(ν_r) ν_r + g(η) = τ
```

- `M_RB = diag(m, m, m, Ix, Iy, Iz)`, exact because the origin is the CG.
- `M_A = diag(A_i)`, the added mass per axis (positive numbers; the literature
  prints them as negative derivatives `X_u̇`...).
- `C_RB(ν) ν = [m ω×v ; ω×(Iω)]`, the rigid-body Coriolis and centripetal terms.
- `C_A(ν_r) ν_r = [ω×(A₁v_r) ; v_r×(A₁v_r) + ω×(A₂ω)]`, Fossen's added-mass
  Coriolis matrix for a diagonal `M_A`, with `A₁ = diag(A₁..A₃)`, `A₂ = diag(A₄..A₆)`.
- `D(ν_r) ν_r`: per axis `(d_lin_i + d_quad_i |ν_r,i|) ν_r,i`.
- `ν_r = ν − [Rᵀ v_c ; 0]`: the velocity relative to the water, `v_c` the current
  in the world frame (constant, or sinusoidal with a period, `sim_current_at`).
  The current is irrotational, so only the linear part is relative; added-mass
  Coriolis and damping act on `ν_r`, the rigid-body terms on `ν`. (The term
  `M_A ν̇_c` of a varying current is dropped: for the slow variation this models
  it is far below the damping.)
- `g(η)`: with `e_b = Rᵀ [0 0 1]ᵀ` (down, in the body), the restoring wrench on the
  right-hand side is `[(W − B) e_b ; cb × (−B e_b)]`; `cb` is the centre of
  buoyancy relative to the CG (above it: `cb[2] < 0`). Computed from the
  quaternion directly, no Euler angles in the dynamics.
- Kinematics: `ṗ = R v`, `q̇ = ½ q ⊗ (0, ω)`.

Integration: classical RK4 over the 13-element state [p q ν] at the caller's step
(`SIM_DEFAULT_DT` = 1/200 s), the quaternion normalized after each step (and in
the working copy of each stage, so a stage's drift from unit length does not
scale the velocity). After the step, depth below 0 is clamped to 0 and the
world-frame vertical velocity zeroed if it points up; the same at
`floor_depth` pointing down. Horizontal and angular motion continue along the
limit. `sim_energy` returns `½ νᵀ(M_RB + M_A)ν + (B − W) z + B (R cb)_z`, which
decays monotonically in still water with no thrust (a test).

`sim_params_add_payload` adds a point mass at a body position: the CG moves
towards it, so `cb` and the thruster positions' frame shift the other way by
`m_p/(m+m_p)` of its offset; the inertia takes the diagonal parallel-axis terms.
This is the model's "payload mass and position" parameter of doc 14; a payload
is assumed to have no buoyancy of its own.

## Parameters and their sources

`sim_params_bluerov2_heavy()`. The hydrodynamic values are the published
heavy-configuration defaults, which are not measurements of a heavy: Wu had no
vehicle and took them from Sandøy's 2016 identification of the standard
BlueROV, setting roll and pitch equal to yaw. Doc 14 says as much: the
simulator is something to calibrate, not to trust, and `hangar sim fit`
replaces buoyancy, damping and lag from a recording.

  Vehicle*, MSc thesis, Flinders University, 2018.

forward 9 kgf (45°), vertical 14 kgf, lateral 9 kgf (45°). Not used by the model.

Things that had to be converted or decided: Wu's negative derivatives are
stored positive; his moment arms in mm are in metres here; his body origin at
the CB is moved to the CG (which makes `M_RB` diagonal exactly and puts the
whole restoring moment into `cb`); his thruster directions are replaced by
ArduSub's, which decide what a pulse width does on the real vehicle.

## The thrust table

`data/t200_thrust.csv`: Blue Robotics, *T200-Public-Performance-Data-10-20V-September-2019.xlsx*,
<https://cad.bluerobotics.com/T200-Public-Performance-Data-10-20V-September-2019.xlsx>,
linked from the T200 product page's "performance charts"; fetched 2026-11-07
(spreadsheet created 2019-09-14, modified 2019-09-24). One sheet per voltage,
10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20 V; the CSV holds each sheet's "Force (Kg f)" column
against "PWM (µs)", 1100 to 1900 µs in 4 µs steps (201 rows), as stored in the
file, negative for reverse. Its header carries the sheet's own test notes: static
bollard thrust, one T200 with a Basic ESC R3, 20 samples at 10 Hz averaged after
1.5 s of settling, readings under 300 RPM dropped to zero (which is what makes
the deadband rows exact zeros: ±36 µs at 12 V, ±28 µs at 16 V). The full data
was fetched; nothing is digitized from a chart.

`data/t200_thrust.inc` is generated from the CSV by `data/gen_t200_inc.sh`
(kgf × 9.80665 → N) and checked in so a build needs only a C compiler;
`test_t200_inc_matches_csv` reads the CSV back and checks every entry.
`sim_t200_thrust(pwm, V)` interpolates linearly in pulse width between the 4 µs
rows and in voltage between the six columns, clamping to [1100, 1900] µs and
[10, 20] V. Doc 14 speaks of one table at the nominal voltage with a voltage
factor; with six published voltages interpolation between them is the better
version of the same idea.

Allocation: `T[6][8]`, column i `= polarity_i [dir_i ; pos_i × dir_i]`; wrench
`τ = T · thrust`. `sim_applied_wrench(pwm[8], voltage, out[6])` runs pulse widths
through the table and `T` with no lag: it is both the steady state of the
simulator's command path (`sim_thrusters_command` / `_step` / `_wrench`, a
first-order lag per thruster stepped exactly) and the depth-hold label's
conversion of `thr.out`; a test compares the two over the whole table.

## Sensor models

`sim_rng` is xorshift64, seeded by the caller; `sim_rng_gauss` is the polar
Box–Muller method, discarding the second value so that the state alone decides
the next draw. A `sim_channel` is noise sd, bias and latency; `sim_delay` is a
fixed ring of 3-vectors (`SIM_DELAY_MAX` = 256 samples, 1.28 s at 200 Hz) that
realizes the latency. `sim_imu_truth` gives body rates and specific force
(`ν̇ + ω×v − Rᵀ g`, so (0, 0, −g) at rest and level); `sim_depth_measure` and
`sim_attitude_measure` read depth and roll/pitch/yaw through a channel. The
`*_typical()` values (gyro 7e-4 rad/s, accel 0.01 m/s², depth 5 mm, attitude
0.5°, latencies 5–20 ms) are **engineering estimates**, not datasheet figures;
`sim_vehicle` exposes each as a parameter.

## The fit

`sim_fit` takes samples of (t, q, ν, pwm[8], voltage) and a baseline
`sim_params`, and returns buoyancy, `d_lin[6]`, `d_quad[6]`, the lag, and per-axis
RMS residuals (the unexplained wrench, in N or N m) with the RMS velocity for
scale. Per axis, `M_ii ν̇_i + C_i + g_i(W, B) + d_lin_i ν_i + d_quad_i |ν_i| ν_i = τ_i(lag)`,
with `ν̇` from central differences and `τ` from the pulse widths through the
table, the lag filter and `T`; the wrench paired with a sample is the mean of
the two held wrenches over the central-difference window, which is what that
mean acceleration answers to (pairing the wrench at the sample itself biases
the lag by half a sample). Everything but the lag is linear in the unknowns,
so for a given lag the 13 unknowns solve one joint normal-equations system
(Gaussian elimination with pivoting, 13×13) with a small ridge towards the
baseline (`ridge × mean diagonal`, default 1e-6): an axis the dive never excited
keeps its baseline value instead of making the system singular. The lag is the
minimizer of the summed squared residual over [0, `lag_max_s`] by golden-section
search (40 iterations). The fit assumes the baseline's current (zero unless
set); a dive in an unknown current biases the linear damping.

The test generates 90 s of a synthetic dive with known parameters (random
thruster commands held for half a second each, recorded at 200 Hz) and recovers
them from the published defaults. Achieved: buoyancy and lag to better than
1e-4 relative, every damping coefficient to better than 0.5%, residuals about
1e-3 N (the central difference's discretization error). The bounds the test
asserts leave a margin for compilers' rounding: 1% on every damping coefficient,
0.02 N on buoyancy, 2% on the lag, residuals under 0.01 N. On a real recording
the residual is dominated by what the model leaves out and by sensor noise, and
the per-axis `rms_residual` against `rms_velocity` is the report's measure of
how far the fitted model is from the vehicle.

## Left out, on purpose (doc 14)

Hull and tether hydrodynamics beyond the diagonal terms (no cross-coupling,
no `M_A` off-diagonals), wall and surface effects (the surface is a hard limit,
not a free surface), current fields that vary in space (one velocity for the
whole world, varying only in time), thruster–thruster and thruster–hull
interaction (bollard thrust applied at the thruster, whatever the flow), the
`M_A ν̇_c` term of a varying current, battery sag under load (voltage is an input),
and the ESC's own dynamics beyond the first-order lag.

## How `sim_vehicle` uses it

Per 200 Hz step: read `thr.cmd` (or SITL's servo outputs) and `power.battery`;
`sim_thrusters_command(th, pwm, voltage)`; `sim_thrusters_step(th, dt)`;
`sim_thrusters_wrench(th, tau)`; `sim_step(params, state, tau, dt)`; publish
`sim.pose` (`state.pos`, `state.q`), `sim.velocity` (`state.nu`), `sim.thrust`
(`th->thrust`). For the sensors: `sim_imu_truth` then `sim_imu_measure` with the
module's `sim_rng` seeded from `--seed`, each truth vector pushed into a
`sim_delay` and read back at the channel's latency; `sim_depth_measure` and
`sim_attitude_measure` at 50 Hz. Parameters that change live (buoyancy,
payload, current, noise) are plain fields of `sim_params` and the channel
structs; `sim_params_add_payload` for a payload. `hangar sim fit` reads a
session's `nav.*`, `thr.out` and `power.battery` into `sim_fit_sample`s (body
velocity reconstructed from the recorded attitude and depth, or from SITL's
truth where available) and calls `sim_fit`; the depth-hold label calls
`sim_applied_wrench(thr_out, voltage, out)` and takes `out[2]`.
